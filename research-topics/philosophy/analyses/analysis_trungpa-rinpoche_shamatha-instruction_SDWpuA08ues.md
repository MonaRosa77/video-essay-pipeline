# Analysis: Shamatha Meditation Instruction -- Breath and Simplicity

- **Video**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SDWpuA08ues
- **Channel**: TrungpaRinpoche
- **Speaker**: Chogyam Trungpa Rinpoche
- **Tradition**: Tibetan Buddhism (Kagyu/Nyingma), Shambhala

## Core Teaching
Trungpa provides detailed, practical shamatha (mindfulness-awareness) meditation instruction. The technique is radically simple: sit with good posture, follow the outbreath as it dissolves into space, let the inbreath happen naturally without attention, and allow thoughts to pass through without labeling them good or bad. He emphasizes that this practice offers no mystical experiences, visions, or "kicks" -- it is "irritatingly down to earth" and its purpose is self-knowledge, not transcendence. The instruction is framed as demanding but accessible, requiring exertion and patience rather than special ability.

## Key Concepts
- **Shamatha (shamata)**: "Development of peace" -- but peace meaning non-action, not political peace or psychedelic bliss; the "phew" of sitting down after exertion
- **Vipashyana**: Mentioned as the second stage but not elaborated here -- the progression from mindfulness to insight
- **Bhavana**: Sanskrit term meaning exertion/discipline -- the foundation without which practice is impossible
- **Outbreath emphasis**: Attention follows the outbreath as it dissolves into space; the inbreath is left alone as natural physiological function -- a distinctive Kagyu/Trungpa instruction
- **Posture**: Straightened spine and neck ("semi relaxed, friendly, seductive, but straightforward"); mouth slightly open as if saying "a"; hands on knees or in meditation posture
- **Thoughts as traffic**: Thoughts are neither good nor bad -- they become like street noise heard through a window, eventually prompting only "so what?"
- **No promises**: Explicitly states there will be no cherubs, heavenly realms, colorful mantras -- the practice reveals "colors of your own existence" not messages from heaven
- **Self-knowledge as goal**: "Realize yourself; understand yourself" -- possibly boring, possibly unwanted, but necessary before one can know what one is

## Notable Quotes
- "Meditation in Buddhism is extremely severe. What we're doing here is extremely traditional, non-trippy, if you'll allow me to use that word."
- "You sat there. You're about to address your lover. You're there; so then just go along with your breathing."
- "Such understanding may be extremely boring; such understandings maybe you don't want to do; but nevertheless that is the case that we can't reject ourselves before we know what we are."
- "I do not promise, on the other hand, that you're not going to see cherubs, gods, heavenly realms, colorful mantras or yantras, none of those."
- "The earth will begin to come back to you; rather than you're getting messages from the heaven as such."

## Philosophical Context
This appears to be from an early Naropa Institute course -- Trungpa mentions it is "the first time I have publicly advised people to sit." The instruction represents standard Kagyu shamatha-vipashyana practice with Trungpa's distinctive emphasis on the outbreath dissolving into space (rather than equal attention to in- and outbreath, as in some Theravada traditions). The "gap" between outbreath and inbreath connects to the Mahamudra emphasis on recognizing awareness in the spaces between thoughts. His insistence on the "non-trippy" and "down to earth" quality is a direct counter to the 1970s spiritual marketplace of psychedelic spirituality, TM, and various guru cults promising instant experiences. The seductive lover analogy for posture is characteristically Trungpa -- grounding formal instruction in embodied, slightly provocative imagery. The introduction of teaching assistants reveals his early pedagogical infrastructure.

## Questions Raised
- The emphasis on outbreath only (letting inbreath be natural) is a specific instruction -- how does this differ from Theravada anapanasati where both in- and outbreath receive equal attention?
- Why the "gap" between breaths? This seems to point toward Mahamudra/Dzogchen recognition of naked awareness -- is shamatha already being infused with higher practice?
- Trungpa says this practice is "extremely severe" and "extremely traditional" -- severe in what sense? The technique sounds gentle. Is the severity in its honesty about what it reveals?
- The claim "this is the first time I have publicly advised people to sit" -- if accurate, when was this? How does it relate to his earlier teaching career in the UK (1960s)?
