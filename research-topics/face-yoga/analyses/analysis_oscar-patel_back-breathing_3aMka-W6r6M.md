# Summary: How Breathing Is Making You Ugly (Back Breathing Technique)

- **Video**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3aMka-W6r6M
- **Channel**: Oscar Patel
- **Topic**: Proper breathing mechanics and their effect on facial development

## Key Points
- Proper breathing involves diaphragm lowering, rib cage expansion, thoracic/cervical fascia release, and tongue elevation against the palate
- The tongue pressing against the roof of the mouth shifts the maxilla upward and forward, influencing overall facial bone development
- Shallow/chest breathing halts developmental processes and keeps the body in a stressed sympathetic state
- "Back breathing" means directing air through the nose backward (horizontally) rather than upward, because the nasal cavity is more spacious horizontally
- Optimal breathing ratio: 4 seconds inhale, 6 seconds exhale to activate parasympathetic nervous system
- Fewer breaths over a lifetime correlates with longevity
- Breathing out during thumb pulling (and in during rest) works with cranial sacral rhythm to decompress sutures

## Method/Routine
1. **Back breathing**: Breathe deeply through nose, imagining funneling air backward into the back of the head/spine rather than upward through the top of the nose
2. **Breath timing**: Inhale for 4 seconds, exhale for 6 seconds as baseline pattern
3. **During thumb pulling**: Exhale while pushing up and out with thumbs; inhale during rest periods
4. Be conscious of breathing throughout the day; this is a habit correction, not a timed exercise
5. Combine with proper tongue posture (tongue on roof of mouth)

## Claims to Verify
- Claim that "the less breaths you take throughout your life, the longer you live" (there is some research on lower resting respiratory rates correlating with longevity, but the statement is oversimplified)
- Claim that back breathing will cause fascia to relax and face to shift "upwards and forwards" (breathing mechanics do affect posture, but direct facial bone changes from breathing direction alone are not well-established in adults)
- Claim that cranial sacral rhythm can be intentionally worked with through breathing timing (craniosacral therapy is considered alternative medicine with limited evidence)
