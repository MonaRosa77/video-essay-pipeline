# Summary: How I Grew 3 Inches After Puberty by Fixing Anterior Pelvic Tilt

- **Video**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vgpdljZQxtY
- **Channel**: Oscar Patel
- **Topic**: Correcting anterior pelvic tilt to gain height and improve posture

## Key Points
- Claims to have grown over 3 inches after puberty (growth plates closed) purely by fixing posture/anterior pelvic tilt
- Over 90% of the population allegedly has compromised posture from sedentary lifestyles
- Uses the "RSS method": Release, Stretch, Strengthen
- Fascia stores tension and prevents muscles from operating properly, leading to bone imbalances and lost height
- Tight muscles to stretch: hip flexors, iliopsoas, rectus femoris, quadriceps, lumbar erectors
- Weak muscles to strengthen: glutes, core/abs
- Emphasizes permanent habit change over temporary fixes — barefoot sprinting, good posture throughout the day

## Method/Routine
1. **Release** (10 min): Full-body fascial release with tennis ball or lacrosse ball — roll from feet to calves, hamstrings, spine, chest, back, neck at ~75% pressure
2. **Stretch** (3 exercises, 2-3 rounds each):
   - Half-kneeling hip flexor stretch (30-60 sec each side)
   - Wall quad stretch (30-60 sec each side)
   - Child's pose in between stretches
3. **Strengthen** (2 exercises, 2-3 sets):
   - Glute bridge/hip thrust — progressive overload with dumbbells; upgrade to feet-elevated version
   - Dead bug exercise — weighted with wrist/ankle weights or bands
4. **Maintenance**: Barefoot sprinting, weighted dead hangs, conscious posture throughout the day, elevated towel scrunches
- Frequency: 2-3 times per week

## Claims to Verify
- "Grew over 3 inches after puberty, after growth plates were closed" — growing 3 inches from posture alone after growth plate closure is an extraordinary claim; while posture correction can restore compressed height, 3 inches is extreme
- "Over 90% of the population has cooked posture" — vague statistic without citation
- Connection between thumb pulling being "temporary" unless tongue maintains pressure — applies orthotropic logic to full-body posture
