# Summary: How to Mew Properly (Complete Guide)

- **Video**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=e2CT5_Wn2yQ
- **Channel**: Oscar Patel
- **Topic**: Comprehensive mewing (tongue posture) technique guide

## Key Points
- Tongue should be suctioned (not pushed) against the roof of the mouth 24/7
- Tongue provides 600g of force; only 1.7g needed to move a tooth — so consistent tongue posture can reshape the palate
- Must mew while sleeping for full-day coverage — sleep on side with pillow between legs
- Tongue tie (lingual frenulum) restricts range of motion and must be loosened through exercises
- Tongue chewing (pushing gum against palate with tongue tip) strengthens genioglossus and styloglossus
- Zygomatic tape and mentalis tape help maintain muscular tension during sleep
- Expanded palate by 8mm and corrected crooked teeth without orthodontics (claimed)

## Method/Routine
1. **Suction mew**: Say letter T → smile wide → swallow → repeat until all saliva gone and tongue is vacuum-suctioned to palate
2. **One-week retraining**: Every hour, practice and hold suction as long as possible
3. **Sleep mewing**: Suction mew before bed; sleep on side with pillow between legs; wear zygomatic + mentalis tape
4. **Tongue tie stretch**: Say T, hold tongue at T-spot, resist backward with finger (30 sec, 2 sets daily, 70% effort)
5. **Tongue chewing**: Chew natural gum, then repeatedly push gum against palate with tongue tip (10 min/day)
6. **Supplement with**: Thumb pulling, myofunctional exercises, proper diet/lifestyle

## Claims to Verify
- "Expanded my palate over 8mm and recorrected my crooked teeth without any orthodontic intervention" — extraordinary claim; self-reported without independent clinical verification
- "1.7 grams of force to move a tooth" — this figure comes from orthodontic literature but refers to sustained orthodontic force, not tongue pressure alone
- "If you genuinely had perfect tongue posture from birth, you would be a supermodel" — vast oversimplification of facial attractiveness factors
- Tongue posture alone correcting sleep apnea and overbite — these conditions typically require medical/orthodontic intervention
