# Summary: 175 Days Fixing My Asymmetry — Full Process

- **Video**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NFSXQtDF5tE
- **Channel**: Oscar Patel
- **Topic**: Comprehensive approach to fixing facial asymmetry through body posture, palate correction, muscle training, and sleep habits

## Key Points
- Facial asymmetry mirrors body posture: lateral pelvic tilt, scoliosis, forward head posture, and flat feet all cascade upward to create facial imbalance
- The body's 600 muscles function as one interconnected system; pulling on one area affects the whole chain
- RSS method (Release, Stretch, Strengthen): release fascia, stretch muscles, then strengthen them — applied to postural issues
- Asymmetrical palate is the core facial issue: one side is larger, so tongue pushes unevenly, developing muscles/bones asymmetrically
- Tongue training (genioglossus/hyoglossus), thumb pulling, and chin tucking address palatal asymmetry
- Muscle-specific training provides fast but temporary results: CPS exercise for orbicularis oris (smile), masseter engagement for jaw, orbicularis oculi for uneven eyes
- Shifting muscles over time also shifts bone through osteoblast generation
- Sleep on your side (not back) — claims tongue can't maintain roof-of-mouth contact when sleeping supine

## Method/Routine
1. Identify postural issues: Thomas test (APT), ASIS height test (lateral pelvic tilt), wet foot test (flat feet), wall posture test (forward head posture)
2. Apply RSS method: release fascia, stretch muscles, strengthen muscles — 15 min routine, 2-3x/week
3. Combine with barefoot sprinting (2x/week) and mace bell training (2x/week)
4. Strengthen tongue muscles: tongue tie stretch, towel tongue stretch, isometric tongue holds
5. Thumb pulling + chin tucking to provide even palatal pressure
6. Train facial muscles: CPS exercise, masseter engagement, orbicularis oculi training
7. Sleep with zygo tape every night on your side (not back)
8. Train neck muscles for cranial support

## Claims to Verify
- Claim that sleeping on your back prevents tongue-to-palate contact — this is debatable; many people maintain tongue posture supine
- Assertion that lateral pelvic tilt "will literally mimic with a crossbite" — the body-to-face connection exists but is overstated as a direct causal chain
- "None of this is genetics" regarding asymmetry — genetics do play a role in facial structure, though habits clearly contribute
- Tongue applying "up to 600g of force" — this figure appears in some literature but varies significantly between studies
