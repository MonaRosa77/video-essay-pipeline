# Summary: How to Lower Your Cortisol

- **Video**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JxiF7xLEIZE
- **Channel**: Oscar Patel
- **Topic**: Lowering chronically elevated cortisol through sleep, light exposure, and structured hydration

## Key Points
- Cortisol is a "master hormone" — when chronically elevated it causes puffy face, low energy, accelerated aging, and poor recovery
- Three sleep non-negotiables: morning sunlight, red light glasses after sunset, magnesium spray on feet before bed
- Morning sun exposure spikes cortisol naturally and signals melatonin production 14 hours later
- Blue light in the evening disrupts circadian rhythm by mimicking morning sunlight signals
- Claims magnesium glycinate supplements are poorly bioavailable; recommends transdermal magnesium spray instead
- "Light diet": 20-30 minutes of full-body morning sunlight activates cytochrome C oxidase, powering ATP production via mitochondria
- "Structured hydration": getting water from foods (watermelon, cucumber, milk, undercooked meat) rather than drinking plain water, which allegedly flushes out minerals

## Method/Routine
1. Sleep fix: morning sunlight exposure, red light glasses after sunset, magnesium spray on feet before bed
2. Light diet: 20-30 min direct full-body sunlight every morning; maximize outdoor time during the day
3. Structured hydration: eat water-rich foods (fruits, milk, raw meat juices) rather than chugging plain water; pair with electrolytes naturally

## Claims to Verify
- Claim that magnesium glycinate supplements are generally not bioavailable — contradicts research showing decent absorption rates for this specific form
- Claim that plain water "flushes out minerals" and causes dehydration — oversimplified; moderate water intake is well-supported by medical consensus
- Assertion that cells become "insulin resistant" without proper light exposure — needs citation
- Cytochrome C oxidase activation from sunlight claim is partially supported (red/NIR light research exists) but overstated for general sunlight
