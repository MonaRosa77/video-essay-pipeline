# Summary: How to Raise a Supermodel (Facial Development from Birth)

- **Video**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Y3fIkeOgv14
- **Channel**: Oscar Patel
- **Topic**: Optimal childhood development practices for facial structure, with applications for adults

## Key Points
- "The biggest difference between an average person and a supermodel isn't genetics, it's habits"
- Four key developmental steps: breastfeeding, crawling/movement, tongue posture, and chewing
- Breastfeeding for 2+ years develops tongue strength and palate shape through peristaltic tongue thrusting
- Formula bottles with air gaps make feeding too easy, preventing tongue strengthening
- Crawling is the foundation of biomechanics — being forced to walk too early prevents proper fascial spiral integration
- Babies mimic parents' habits/expressions ("facial contiguity principle" attributed to Dr. John Mew)
- Closing baby's mouth triggers instinctive tongue-to-palate mechanism
- Chewing tough foods (especially animal foods) as early as possible develops the face through mechanotransduction

## Method/Routine
**For babies/children:**
1. Breastfeed for at least 2 years (tongue thrusting develops palate)
2. Allow extended crawling period — do not rush walking with contraptions
3. Encourage barefoot movement from early age
4. Close baby's mouth to trigger tongue-to-palate reflex; avoid pacifiers
5. Introduce tough foods (meat, animal foods) for chewing as early as able
6. Parents should model proper habits (closed mouth, good posture) — children mimic

**For adults (same principles, different execution):**
1. Tongue stretches and myofunctional exercises to retrain swallowing
2. Slant board exercises and isometric foot holds for anterior tibial tendon
3. Mewing (tongue suction to palate)
4. Forward bilateral chewing with tough foods
5. Nasal breathing (backwards into face)

## Claims to Verify
- "Over 95% of modern babies are at least partially formula-fed" — US CDC data shows ~84% start breastfeeding, ~60% are still breastfeeding at 6 months; 95% partial formula stat needs citation
- "Facial contiguity principle" and adopted children resembling parents through habits — interesting concept but evidence is limited/anecdotal
- Weston Price's work on diet and facial development — real researcher but his conclusions are debated in modern nutrition science
- "The only difference between a normal person and a supermodel is millimeters" — oversimplification that ignores many factors
- Claim that adults can achieve comparable results to children — the degree of skeletal remodeling possible in adults is much more limited
