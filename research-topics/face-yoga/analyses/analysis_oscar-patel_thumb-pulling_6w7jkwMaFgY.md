# Summary: Thumb Pulling 2.0 Full Guide (Palate Expansion Technique)

- **Video**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6w7jkwMaFgY
- **Channel**: Oscar Patel
- **Topic**: Technique for expanding the palate and loosening cranial sutures using thumbs

## Key Points
- Thumb pulling loosens fascia and cranial sutures to allow the face to develop properly when combined with correct tongue posture
- The skull has 22 bones connected by sutures that are meant to be malleable and adaptive
- Thumb pulling 2.0 emphasizes fascial release over static holds; the key is a chin-tuck position against a wall for stabilization
- The mid-palatal suture is the most important target; push upwards and outwards
- Results are temporary unless combined with proper tongue posture (suction mewing) which acts as a natural retainer
- Three-minute sessions repeated 2-3 times daily; each rep should last only 8-12 seconds of intense pressure
- Creaking sounds during the technique indicate sutures loosening (similar to NCR therapy)

## Method/Routine
1. **Loosen fascia first**: Zygo pulling (massage under cheekbone upward/outward), palate massage, tongue stretching, neck stretching, cobra pose
2. **Position**: Stand against wall, feet 6 inches forward, chin tucked fully to neck
3. **Thumb pulling 2.0**: Place thumbs in middle of palate; push upward and outward while maintaining chin tuck; hold intensely for 8-12 seconds until chin lifts off neck
4. **Fascial release**: When chin rises, stop resisting with neck but maintain thumb pressure and drive upward
5. **Timer**: Set 3-minute timer; repeat the movement continuously (approximately 15-18 reps per session)
6. **Three positions**: Middle of palate (priority), front (behind incisors), back (before wisdom teeth area)
7. Perform 2-3 times daily; combine with suction mewing throughout the day for permanent results

## Claims to Verify
- Claim that cranial sutures remain malleable enough in adults to be shifted by thumb pressure (sutures begin fusing in late adolescence; in adults over 25, significant movement is unlikely without surgical intervention)
- Claim that creaking sounds indicate sutures loosening (could also be cartilage, soft tissue, or other structures)
- Comparison to NCR (neurocranial restructuring) which is itself an unproven alternative therapy
- Claim that the tongue acts as a "natural retainer" that can maintain palate width changes (tongue posture affects development primarily during growth years)
