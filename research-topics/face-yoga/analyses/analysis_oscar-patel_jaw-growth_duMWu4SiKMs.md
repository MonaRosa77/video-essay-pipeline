# Summary: How to Grow Your Jaw Forward (MFP Method)

- **Video**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=duMWu4SiKMs
- **Channel**: Oscar Patel
- **Topic**: Three-step method to grow the jaw forward (muscles, fascia, passive habits)

## Key Points
- Presents the "MFP Method": Muscles, Fascia, Passive habits
- Forward jaw projection correlates with better posture, breathing, and longevity
- Muscles directly influence bone structure through mechanical tension generating osteoblasts
- Three key muscles: masseter (develop it), digastric (activate it), mentalis (deactivate it)
- Fascia must be loosened for muscles to stretch and results to become permanent
- Breathing pattern of 4 seconds in, 6 seconds out is recommended as a baseline
- All corrective exercises should be temporary (a few months); permanent results come from good passive habits

## Method/Routine
1. **Develop masseters**: Chew harder foods (meat); practice frowning while chewing for mind-muscle connection (1-2 min/day); massage jaw to relieve tension
2. **Activate digastric muscle**: Use breathing resistance device, balloon, or deep nasal breathing in mewing position
3. **Deactivate mentalis**: Wear X-shaped mentalis tape on chin
4. **Loosen fascia — tongue stretch**: Stick tongue out, hold 10 sec forward, 10 sec left, 10 sec right, repeat twice
5. **Loosen fascia — thumb pulling + chin tucking**: Thumbs in palate to loosen mid-palatal suture
6. **Fix passive habits**: Maintain 4-in/6-out breathing pattern; do breathwork 2-3 times per week; maintain proper chewing, swallowing, breathing, and posture

## Claims to Verify
- "Grew my jaw over 15mm" — extraordinary claim without independent verification or imaging evidence shown
- Mechanical tension from masseters generating osteoblasts that change adult bone structure — while Wolff's law applies to bone remodeling, the extent of facial bone change from chewing in adults is debated
- "If you actually chewed properly... this will change your face in a matter of weeks" — timeline claim seems aggressive for structural change
- 4-in/6-out breathing loosening sutures — no evidence that breathing patterns directly affect cranial sutures
