# Summary: 4 Years of Mewing — Key Lessons Learned

- **Video**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HijEkS6Yhu8
- **Channel**: Oscar Patel
- **Topic**: Four major lessons from 4 years of mewing practice

## Key Points
- **Lesson 1**: Tongue can change your entire face — 600g of force capability; maxilla rotation counterclockwise shifts face upward/forward
- **Lesson 2**: Tongue is the driver but not the catalyst — modern lifestyle has constricted fascia, narrowed palates; ancestors had wider palates
- **Lesson 3**: Tongue posture affects entire body through fascia — tongue, feet, and hands are the three key fascial points
- **Lesson 4**: Breathing technique is essential for mewing to work — shallow breathing hardens sutures, keeps body in sympathetic state
- Must mew 24/7 including sleep; set 6+ daily alarms as reminders during retraining
- Recommends Buteyko or Vortex breathing + "back breathing"

## Method/Routine
1. **Suction mew**: Say T → smile wide → swallow → keep swallowing until tongue is vacuum-sealed to palate
2. **Set 6+ alarms** throughout the day to remind yourself of tongue posture
3. **Sleep mewing**: Sleep on side with pillow between legs; wear zygo tape; suction mew before bed
4. **Breathing practice** (daily or 3+ times/week): Choose Buteyko breathing or Vortex breathing
5. **Back breathing**: Breathe backwards into your back (not upward into head) — releases thoracic fascia
6. **Support techniques**: Thumb pulling, body posture alignment, loosening fascia, activating muscles

## Claims to Verify
- "Our ancestors had 50mm palates" — some archaeological evidence supports wider palates in pre-agricultural societies but 50mm as universal norm is unverified
- Tongue posture providing "neurological signal to the proprioceptive feedback loop" that cascades throughout the body — theoretical framework, not established neuroscience
- "Back breathing" releasing cranial base and thoracic fascia — breathing mechanics affect thoracic mobility but claims about cranial base decompression are speculative
- Athletes having tongue on roof of mouth at higher rates — presented as correlation without data
